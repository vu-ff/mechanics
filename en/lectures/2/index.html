Lecture 2

Constraints and Lagrange’s equations

What this lecture does

Lecture 1 gave the state and the flow of a system whose particles move freely. Rods, hinges and rigid bodies constrain them: the allowed configurations form a surface in the space of particle positions. This lecture asks what Newton’s law becomes on such a surface. It says when constraint equations define one, what its velocities are and which forces a constraint may exert, and proves that Newton’s law with those forces is equivalent to Lagrange’s equations for L=T−V\Lag=T-VL=T−V, in any coordinates on the surface, with (q,q˙)(q,\dot q)(q,q˙​) as the state. The pendulum, now with its rod, is the example.
