Lecture 1

States, determinism and phase portraits

What this lecture does

This course asks one question: what is a classical mechanical system? It answers three times, each answer a layer deeper, and the question splits into three: what is a state, what is an observable, and how do they evolve? This lecture gives the first, provisional answers, starting from a question about prediction: what do we need to know about a system now in order to know its future? The answer is the state; the states form the phase space, and the law of motion becomes a flow on it. The lecture states the theorems on differential equations that make this honest, proves that the flow of a particle in a potential bounded below exists for all time, and reads the whole motion of a system with one degree of freedom off the graph of its potential, with the pendulum as the example. It ends with a question the course will answer: what forbids the attractors that friction makes?

1.1 Determinacy

Newton’s principle

A system of NNN point particles in space is described, at each instant, by the positions r1,…,rN∈R3\mathbf r_1,\dots,\mathbf r_N\in\R^{3}r1​,…,rN​∈R3. 1.6 Why phase space, and a contrast

Why phase space

In configuration space, or in configuration space and time, curves cross: the motions of Figure 1.1 meet again at t=πt=\pit=π, and a pendulum passes the bottom going both ways. In phase space they cannot: by Corollary 1.6 through every state there is exactly one orbit. That is determinacy made geometric, and it is why a phase portrait is a family of non-intersecting curves while trajectories in space are a tangle.

Question. What property of the flows of mechanics — as opposed to the damped pendulum — forbids attractors?

Energy conservation already rules out asymptotically stable equilibria: a nearby state of different energy can never reach the equilibrium’s energy. It says less about other attractors, such as limit cycles. A better hint is area. The frictionless flow shears a blob of initial states but keeps its area; the damped flow shrinks it, at the rate given by the divergence of the field, 000 against −γ-\gamma−γ.
