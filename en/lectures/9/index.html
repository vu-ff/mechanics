Lecture 9

Hamilton–Jacobi theory and action–angle variables

What this lecture does

Lecture 8 ended with a question. A time-dependent generating function F2F_2F2​ turns Hamilton’s equations for HHH into Hamilton’s equations for the new Hamiltonian K=H+∂F2/∂tK=H+\partial F_2/\partial tK=H+∂F2​/∂t, so if some F2F_2F2​ made KKK vanish, the new coordinates would be constant and the motion would be solved. This lecture answers the question. The condition on F2F_2F2​ is one first-order partial differential equation, the Hamilton–Jacobi equation, and a family of its solutions with nnn parameters gives the motions themselves (Jacobi’s theorem). Separating the variables is how such families are found, and for a central force the parameters it finds are the energy and the angular momentum. In one degree of freedom the method works on a whole region of closed orbits at once: it yields the action and the angle, in which every oscillation becomes a uniform rotation and the period is 2π2\pi2π times the slope of the action. For the pendulum this explains why the period of Lecture 1 diverges at the separatrix while the action does not.
