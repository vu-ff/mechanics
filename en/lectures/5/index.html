Lecture 5

Observables and the Poisson algebra

What this lecture does

Lecture 4 gave Hamilton’s equations. This lecture asks how an observable — anything one can measure on a state, a function on phase space — changes along a motion. The answer is its bracket with the Hamiltonian, and the Poisson bracket makes the observables an algebra. The lecture proves the bracket’s properties, above all the Jacobi identity; turns conservation laws into bracket equations, so that conserved quantities form a Lie algebra; computes the angular-momentum algebra so(3)\so(3)so(3); and describes time evolution as a motion of the observables themselves. The observables are the first piece of the course’s second answer.

Definition 5.7 (Poisson algebra). A Poisson algebra has two products tied by Leibniz: the product is how devices combine, and the bracket is how they change in time.
