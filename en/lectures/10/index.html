Lecture 10

Momentum maps and reduction

What this lecture does

Separating the central force in Lecture 9 produced the constants L3L_3L3​ and ∣L∣2\abs{\Lvec}^{2}∣L∣2, and they came from the rotations. This lecture turns a whole group of canonical symmetries into conserved quantities at once: the momentum map, a vector of observables whose flows are the directions of the group. For a symmetry of positions it is Noether’s charge p⋅ξp\cdot\xip⋅ξ, and for rotations it is L=q×p\Lvec=q\times pL=q×p. The conserved quantities then reduce the motion: a cyclic variable removes a degree of freedom, and the central force becomes a motion in one degree of freedom. Kepler’s problem has one more conserved vector, which no symmetry of positions produces, and it closes the bounded orbits.
