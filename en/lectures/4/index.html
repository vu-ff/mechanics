Lecture 4

The Legendre transform and Hamilton’s equations

What this lecture does

Lecture 3 gave the first answer: a motion makes the action stationary, and its momentum is p=∂L/∂q˙p=\partial\Lag/\partial\dot qp=∂L/∂q˙​. So far a state has been a position with a velocity. This lecture asks when the velocity can be traded for the momentum. The momentum is a different kind of object, a covector, and the trade is the Legendre transform; it works when the Lagrangian is hyperregular, and then Lagrange’s nnn second-order equations become Hamilton’s 2n2n2n first-order equations on the space of positions and momenta, the phase space, with an action principle of their own. This is the rung from the Lagrange layer to the Hamilton layer, on which the rest of the course stands.
